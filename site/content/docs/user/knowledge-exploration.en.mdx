## Find a source you can check

Open Knowledge Exploration from the sidebar and enter a specific term, such as “derivative”. Public search sends the query to Wikipedia and Wikibooks. Selected open textbook passages are also available offline for matching topics.

Use an encyclopedia for definitions, a textbook for context and examples, or a community source for another explanation. Each card identifies the source. Follow its link to inspect the original notation and conditions. Wikipedia and Wikibooks share an operator.

Choose local materials to search text-based PDFs and lecture notes you have imported and enabled for retrieval. This search runs on your device. Scanned pages need text extraction before they can be searched.

## Optional Zhihu sources

Connect your Zhihu Access Secret under Settings → Knowledge sources. Choose Zhihu in the exploration workspace or turn on Online in a chat to bring in public Zhihu discussions.

## Reading a comparison

Source explanations carry citations. Author views and AI inferences are identified separately. Check whether two sources address the same question under the same conditions. Search returns excerpts, so open the original when a formula or its context is missing. A local math check applies to the calculation it identifies.

## Choosing your next resource

Open your learning profile to see material for your current goal. Tell MathTutor how much time you have, what you have already read and what you want to try on your own. The app distinguishes independent attempts, hints and reading. If a prerequisite still needs checking, the plan gives you a starting point. Expand a resource to read its selection reason, excerpt review scope and original link.

Startup discovery is optional in Knowledge sources. With the network offline, saved materials and learning records remain available. The reading workbench also lets you save a reflection and check your understanding on the topics it covers.
